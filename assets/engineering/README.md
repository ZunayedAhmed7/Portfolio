# Engineering I Admire — artwork and references

The current gallery has four studies in this order: Concorde, Lockheed SR-71,
Apollo Guidance Computer, and Falcon 9 landing booster. Drawings are illustrative
and not to scale.

## Current artwork

- `concorde.png`: **Concorde v1.0**, Julien.scavini, February 16, 2012.
  [Original file and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Concorde_v1.0.png).
  Licensed [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).
  Original PNG bytes are unchanged. Author, source, and license links appear on
  the slide.
- `sr-71.svg`: **Lockheed SR-71A 3view**, United States Air Force, distributed
  through [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lockheed_SR-71A_3view.svg).
  Listed there as a US federal government public-domain work. Original SVG bytes
  are unchanged; source and authorship are credited on the slide.
- `apollo-dsky.svg`: unchanged copy of the supplied Apollo DSKY artwork
  (original filename `appolo.svg`).
  Its embedded description credits **Oona Räisänen and NASA** and identifies it as
  a monochrome adaptation of the public-domain
  [Apollo DSKY interface](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Apollo_DSKY_interface.svg).
  This is the display and keyboard interface to the guidance computer.
- `falcon-9-recovery-detail.svg`: the displayed crop of the supplied
  `Falcon_9_Reusability_Line_Art.svg`, matching the requested framing of the return
  trajectory and landing. Its embedded description identifies a source-derived
  line-art adaptation of **SpaceX Photos' 2015 reusability graphic**,
  dedicated to the public domain under
  [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).
  [Original graphic and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Falcon_9_First_Stage_Reusability_Graphic.jpg).
  Its SVG viewport is `900 45 1733 1400`. Only the root width, height, and viewBox
  differ from the supplied source. Every artwork path and embedded source
  metadata element is preserved in this file, so a duplicate full-view file is
  not bundled.

The website applies a CSS color filter to present the dark lines in the blueprint
palette. Supplied SVG geometry, annotations, and source metadata are preserved.

## Captions

- Concorde's speed, record Atlantic crossing, and retirement are checked against
  [British Airways' history and facts](https://hotline.britishairways.com/content/information/about-ba/history-and-heritage/celebrating-concorde).
  The personal Atlantic crossing twenty years after retirement comes directly
  from the owner's notes; no particular flight route or airport pair is added.
- SR-71's technical context comes from
  [NASA's overview](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/sr-71-3/).
- Apollo's role and computing constraints are checked against
  [NASA's Apollo 11 guidance-computer account](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11.1201-fm.html).
  The caption specifies the first **crewed** Moon landing and reflects the owner's
  interest in achieving so much with limited computing resources.
- Falcon's recovery context uses
  [SpaceX's vehicle reference](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/falcon-9/) and the
  supplied trajectory artwork. The owner's notes provide the personal interest
  in reusing a large, complex vehicle and the math and computing behind precision
  landing. No unsupported performance figures are added.
